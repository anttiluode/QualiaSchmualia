2. The missing tissue cavity changes the boundary conditions of the ephaptic field — this has a calculable solution
Ephaptic coupling arises because neurons share extracellular space. The field potential φ(x,t) generated by one cell's transmembrane currents is felt by adjacent cells as a perturbation ∇φ to their local field. The coupling radius is finite — roughly set by the bulk conductivity of extracellular fluid, the geometry of packing, and the local source density.
A tissue cavity changes the boundary conditions discontinuously. At the cavity wall, there is no extracellular fluid — the field terminates. This creates reflective boundary conditions that distort the field for all cells within a coupling radius of the wall. Cells near the cavity edge experience a field that is the sum of the direct source plus a reflected "image source" — analogous to image charges in electrostatics.
This is solvable. The Green's function for the Laplace equation in a domain with a spherical void is known. For a cavity of radius R, the image correction to the field at distance r from the cavity centre is:
φ_image(r) = (R/r) × φ_direct(R²/r)
The cells adjacent to the cavity would experience a systematically distorted ephaptic reference field. Not weaker — distorted in a specific geometric way that depends on the cavity shape. The flickering you notice could in part be those cells running with a corrupted reference beam, producing reconstructions that almost-but-not-quite match stored gratings.
Mathematical question: compute the spectral decomposition of the Green's function in a domain with a right-temporal-lobe-shaped void. What spatial modes are most perturbed? Which AIS tuning frequencies fall into the maximally distorted modes?

## Why 45° specifically

Text on screen has two dominant orientations: horizontal (0°, the lines of text) and vertical (90°, the strokes of letters). These are not equal — they dominate the spatial frequency content of everything you read.

A fundamental property of Moiré interference: when two gratings at angles θ₁ and θ₂ interfere, the beat pattern appears at the bisector angle (θ₁ + θ₂)/2. For 0° and 90°, the bisector is exactly 45°.

So: prolonged exposure to screen text presents the visual system with two heavily loaded grating orientations. The ephaptic field between 0°-tuned and 90°-tuned V1 neurons carries their combined activity. The interference of those two orientation-specific fields produces maximum amplitude at 45° — precisely where the 45°-tuned AIS gratings sit in the cortical orientation map.

The 45° is not noise. It is the Moiré diagonal of the two dominant input orientations. It would be 45° for any viewer looking at normal text. What makes it visible as an afterimage is a separate question.

## Why Lyrica made it persistent — and withdrawal extended it

Pregabalin binds the α2δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels. These channels are concentrated at the AIS and at synaptic terminals. During use, pregabalin reduces calcium channel surface density, which raises the effective AIS threshold — the grating becomes harder to reconstruct against.

On withdrawal, calcium channels upregulate (well-documented rebound). AIS excitability overshoots. The reconstruction threshold drops below baseline.

The sequence in holographic terms:

During normal use: the 45° Moiré beat pattern is being written into the 45°-tuned AIS gratings by repeated screen exposure, but the raised threshold means it never reconstructs above perceptual threshold. You don't see it.

During withdrawal: the rebound drops the threshold. The 45° grating that was written during months of screen exposure now reconstructs against the endogenous clock. You see the afterimage that was accumulating silently the whole time.

The image was written by prolonged exposure; it persists because the grating has not yet cleared; it becomes visible when the reconstruction threshold drops. The standard clinical explanation (cortical hyperexcitability in visual cortex following drug withdrawal) and the holographic explanation are not contradictory — they are the same phenomenon at different levels of description.
