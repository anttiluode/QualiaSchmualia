Against CEMI, for local ephaptic
The strong-field argument you make is correct and I think it is decisive against the bulk CEMI interpretation.
External TMS fields, transcranial DC stimulation, even the fields inside MRI scanners — these are enormous compared to the brain's own fields, and they do not produce the qualitative richness or global disruption that CEMI would predict. They produce phosphenes (simple, low-dimensional attractors forced locally in visual cortex), they can disrupt specific cognitive functions, they can alter mood — but they do not fundamentally rewrite the character of experience the way that, say, 5-HT2A activation does.
The reason is scale. The relevant ephaptic field is local — acting over ~100 micrometers between adjacent cells, structured at the scale of individual AIS domains. External fields at that scale would need to be extraordinarily spatially precise and frequency-matched to interfere constructively with the local ephaptic patterns. Bulk fields are spatially incoherent at the scale that matters. McFadden's CEMI field is real — the brain does generate a coherent EM field measurable as EEG, and it does feed back into neural firing. The bulk EEG field varies over centimeters; it cannot resolve the difference between a T³ attractor and a T⁵ attractor at the level of individual cortical columns. The local ephaptic field can, because it operates at the scale of the AIS grating spacing itself.
So: McFadden is right that the field matters. But the seat of qualia is not in the bulk field — it is in the local field, at the AIS interface, in the Moiré zone.
